Meeting Recap · August 9, 2026

A Meeting of Missions: Billion Buddhas and Maitri Parivar Come Together

A Sunday video call brought together voices from two spiritual missions to ask a simple question: what could we build if we stopped comparing paths and started walking together?

Namita ji and Srikanth ji opened the call with a warm welcome, bringing together a small circle of people from different corners of the same larger family of seekers. The gathering included Ramanji (Ramen Chakravarthy), Jayanti bhai, Ankita ji, Rita ji, Nisha ji, Usha ji, and others who joined in.

Who Was There Namita ji & Srikanth ji (hosts) · Ramanji (Ramen Chakravarthy) · Jayanti bhai · Ankita ji · Rita ji · Nisha ji · Usha ji

Two Journeys, One Direction

Ramanji shared his own path: a serious personal journey that led him to an awakening in 2012, and later to founding Billion Buddhas — a movement he restarted about a year ago, now setting up a centre in Bangalore. He spoke of being connected to the lineage of Mahavatar Babaji, and was candid that he does not see himself as "a guru sitting on top." His words were direct: this isn't a time for hierarchies, it's a time to collaborate.

Namita ji and Srikanth ji spoke of their own journey with Maitri Parivar and Maitreya Dadashreeji over the past seven to eight years. Their path, too, passed through the writings connected to Mahavatar Babaji, and led them to Dadashreeji — whom they describe as a direct disciple of Babaji. Through a practice they call Maitri Shakti Prava, they came to feel that Babaji's energy and Dadashreeji's energy are one and the same.

"Our paths are different, but the goal is singular."

No Guru, Only a Friend

A recurring thread through the conversation: neither Ramanji nor Dadashreeji wants to be seen as a master or guru in the traditional sense. Dadashreeji, as Srikanth ji explained, asks to be seen simply as a "divine friend" — and the word Maitreya itself points to that idea of friendship. The aim isn't enlightenment as an endpoint, but something the group called Maitri consciousness, or Amrit Avastha — a state described as beyond enlightenment, where love is not just realized but lived at every moment.

Each one of us, if we get transformed, a thousand people around us will get transformed automatically. The responsibility starts and stops with our own transformation.

A Circle That Grows Outward

Srikanth ji described a system sometimes called Maitri ke Das Hath — a small circle of close disciples who relay guidance outward in tiers, since Dadashreeji works mostly on what they describe as the astral plane and meets people physically only rarely. From time to time, simple shared practices are passed along — things like a collective mother-earth prayer — with the belief that when even a small, aligned group (the number 10,000 came up more than once) focuses the same intention at the same time, it measurably shifts the collective consciousness around it, starting with India and rippling outward to the world.

Toward a Shared Horizon

Ramanji spoke about scaling Billion Buddhas well beyond English-speaking audiences — into Japanese, Chinese, South American languages and beyond — deliberately open-sourced, without claiming personal ownership, so that it can spread on its own. Both sides of the call returned again and again to the same target date: 2032, a point by which they hope the wider shift in human consciousness will have taken firmer root.

The call closed on a practical note: starting a shared WhatsApp group to stay connected, taking small joined steps together (starting with something as simple as praying for the earth at the same time), and a shared agreement to keep listening to what Maitreya Dadashreeji's guidance asks of them next.

Above all, what came through was warmth — gratitude between old friends and new ones, and a shared sense that two different missions had quietly been walking toward the same place all along.
